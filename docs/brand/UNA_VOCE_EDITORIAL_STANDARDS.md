# Una Voce Editorial Standards

**Status:** Approved project-wide baseline  
**Applies to:** Public copy, social media, campaign pages, UI text, captions, documentation intended for publication, and final-copy comments

## Brand promise

Una Voce helps Catholics discover the Liturgy of the Hours, find an approachable way to pray, begin with confidence, and keep going with the Church.

The name means **one voice**. It points toward the prayer of the whole Church, offered by different people in different places through one shared daily rhythm.

## Voice

Una Voce should sound:

- Clear.
- Warm.
- Grounded.
- Confident.
- Invitational.
- Occasionally surprising.
- Simple enough for newcomers.
- Deeply familiar with the Liturgy of the Hours.

The writing may be beautiful, but it should never become vague. It may be reverent, but it should never become sentimental or self-important.

## Avoid

- Corporate or product-marketing language.
- Generic inspirational Catholic copy.
- Excessive piety language.
- Dense formation language when a plain explanation will work.
- Cute or trendy phrasing.
- Shame, pressure, or spiritual performance.
- Treating prayer as content consumption.
- Treating prayer as a streak, challenge completion, or engagement mechanic.
- Claims of partnership, endorsement, authority, or ownership that have not been established.

## Writing principles

### Lead with the human invitation

Begin with the experience a person can understand:

- Start with one Hour.
- You are not praying alone.
- Choose the Hour that fits the life you actually have.
- We are praying Morning Prayer together this week.

Explain systems and features only after the human meaning is clear.

### Teach without overwhelming

Give newcomers enough understanding to care and enough clarity to act. A carousel may teach. A caption should add context without repeating every slide.

### Use confidence without pressure

Present the Liturgy of the Hours as a real invitation into the prayer of the Church. Do not imply that a beginner must pray every Hour or maintain a perfect record.

### Keep the Church visible

Una Voce is a doorway and discovery layer. It should point toward the prayer of the Church and the communities that make trusted prayer pathways available.

## Theological and pastoral precision

- Refer to the Liturgy of the Hours as the daily prayer of the Church.
- Explain that clergy and religious pray the Hours and lay Catholics are invited to participate.
- Do not imply that all Catholics have the same obligation to pray every Hour.
- Preserve source attribution and the identity of ministries providing prayer resources.
- Do not reproduce official liturgical text unless the necessary permission or license has been established.
- Describe Devotions as shared invitations into a period of prayer, not measured activation campaigns.
- Discuss participation data only after the pastoral purpose is clear.

## Naming and capitalization

Use:

- `Una Voce`
- `Liturgy of the Hours`
- `the Hours`
- `Hour` when referring to a specific canonical period of prayer
- `Morning Prayer`
- `Evening Prayer`
- `Night Prayer`
- `Lauds`
- `Vespers`
- `Compline`
- `Devotion` when referring to the Una Voce communal prayer model

Use **one voice** as the plain-English meaning of Una Voce.

## Punctuation

### No em dashes

Do not use em dashes in new or revised final copy.

Use one of the following instead:

- A period.
- A comma.
- A colon.
- A semicolon.
- Parentheses.
- A sentence break.

This is a standing Una Voce rule. Legacy material may be updated as it is revised, but new work should follow the rule immediately.

## Social writing

### Carousel copy

- Give each slide one clear job.
- Favor short, memorable statements.
- Vary pacing between provocation, breathing room, explanation, visual emphasis, and invitation.
- Keep supporting copy readable at phone size.
- Let the headline carry the slide when it can.

### Captions

Use this general structure:

1. Conversational opening.
2. One or two useful explanatory paragraphs.
3. Simple invitation.
4. Restrained hashtags when needed.

Do not simply repeat the carousel.

## Visual expression

The voice and visual system should reinforce each other.

Prefer:

- Editorial typography.
- Confident negative space.
- Liturgical line work and diagrams.
- Restrained color and ornament.
- A clear reading order.
- Composition that feels authored.

Avoid:

- Generic Catholic template packs.
- SaaS campaign graphics.
- Glossy simulated religious objects.
- Generic people icons.
- Decorative density without purpose.

For full visual and production guidance, use the [Instagram Visual Guide](UNA_VOCE_INSTAGRAM_VISUAL_GUIDE.md).

## Final copy check

Before approving public copy, ask:

1. Is the human invitation clear?
2. Is the language accurate and understandable to a newcomer?
3. Does it sound pastoral rather than corporate?
4. Does it avoid pressure, performance, and gamification?
5. Are relationships and permissions described precisely?
6. Are all em dashes removed?
7. Does it still sound unmistakably like Una Voce?
